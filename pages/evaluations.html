zensim 0.3.0 profile A: peer comparisons, scatter plots, and runtime benchmarks

zensim-a is a full-reference perceptual image similarity metric built for codec quality control, encoder steering, and visual regression testing. It converts both images to XYB across four dyadic scales, extracts 492 local damage features plus 492 reference-fragility products, and feeds a 128-unit network certified monotone in its damage inputs before a 21-knot monotone PCHIP spline (100 = identical, below 0 for severe damage).

This page compares frozen A primarily against published external full-reference metrics (SSIMULACRA2, Butteraugli, FSIMc / FSIM, HaarPSI, and ColorVideoVDP, plus registry fast-ssim2 in runtime benchmarks). In the shipped unpruned bake, one tied max pair carries 99.998% of the mean raw score contribution (approximately two additive damage budgets, plus a 31-unit residual with output weight −0.000031). Monotonicity holds in the damage features up to floating-point rounding, not across every pixel edit or close encoder step. The per-region steering map (compute_with_diffmap) uses a separate fixed 156-coefficient projection.

4. Per-distortion breakdown vs peers: all 25 KADID-10k TERMINAL types

Each distortion code (01–25) has 80 pairs across 16 reference images (10,000 reference resamples, seed 20261105; 16-reference percentile intervals are descriptive and may under-cover). Codes are canonical from the cached evaluation keys; human-readable labels follow KADID-10k’s imdist_generator.m specification. A leads SSIMULACRA2 and Butteraugli across most blur, noise, and color-shift types (for example 04 Color diffusion 0.922 vs 0.720 / 0.340 and 18 Mean shift 0.808 vs 0.659 / 0.365), while SSIMULACRA2 leads on 21 Pixelate (0.684 vs 0.577) and local patch/block edits remain hard for every metric.

5. Engineering qualification gates, JPEG XL steering, and HDR front end

Rank correlation on MOS collections does not verify dial monotonicity, SIMD determinism, or spatial quantization steering. Model selection enforced five engineering gates alongside human-rating panels; all imperfect pass rates and byte costs against external judges are retained below.

Dial & determinism gates

Certified-monotone head + ladder checks

Dial contract C1–C6 (monotone along ladders, no flat zones, negative tail, exact 100 on identical images, nothing above an identical copy) passed across seeds 0–2 on both evaluation grids, and exact identity passed across 12 x86 SIMD tier permutations. On near-identity ladders, 132 / 144 were monotone per seed. Across 646,677 held-out close encoder-ladder pairs where Butteraugli and CVVDP agree, A still misorders 3.39% of pairs (prior recipe F3: 3.35%).

Spatial maps & JPEG XL loop

156-coefficient map under independent judges

Chroma level (worst MAE ratio 1.008 vs bar 1.05) and chroma trade misordering (2.47% vs F3 2.50%) passed. Seed 0 passed 133 / 135 regional map-steering cases (one text-screenshot case read 0.69 against a 0.70 bar at every seed). Against a Butteraugli-steered JPEG XL quantization loop, zensim’s map costs +2.18% bytes under SSIMULACRA2 and +5.31% under Butteraugli (against F3, the reported byte-delta intervals include zero: +0.19% [−0.09, +0.46] by SSIMULACRA2 and −0.21% [−0.56, +0.12] by Butteraugli).

Zero-shot HDR continuity

Anchored cube root at 203 cd/m² SDR white

compute_hdr maps linear, PQ, or HLG light through an anchored cube-root front end without HDR human training rows. An SDR image presented as HDR at 203 cd/m² scores within 0.0003 points of its SDR score on the research panel. On UPIQ, zero-shot A reads SROCC 0.943 on the 50 development rows (vs 0.835 with an earlier PU21 front end) and 0.811 across all 380 UPIQ rows. HDR scores are not a calibrated human scale.

6. Runtime latency, parallel cutoff, and process memory

Measured on an Intel Core Ultra 7 270K Plus (x86-64 AVX2/v3, CPUs 0–15: 8P + 8E, no CCD boundary): 20 real images per size, zenjpeg q40 YCbCr 4:2:0 reconstructions, resident RGB8 sRGB bytes scored in-process (decode, encode, resize, I/O, and CLI startup excluded), 100 ms warmup, first 32 clean interleaved zenbench rounds per image (640 calls per arm/cell), type-7 quantiles, thin LTO, no -C target-cpu=native. Comparators are registry fast-ssim2 0.9.1 (published Rust SSIMULACRA2) and the in-process margarine simd-row-malta Butteraugli-family kernel (primary max pooling; runtime baseline, not an accuracy comparator). Does not measure Zen 5 AVX-512, ARM NEON, Wasm, or GPU.

Pre-cutoff small-image caveat: All eight comparative rows below were measured before shipping the 36,864-padded-pixel (192²) serial cutoff. At 64², A is slower than fast-ssim2 at 1 thread (0.75 ms vs 0.40 ms) and suffered Rayon overhead at 16 threads (2.15 ms pre-cutoff; 0.70 ms after forcing serial below 192²). At 12 MP (12.00–12.58M pixels), 16-thread A scores in 105.4 ms p50 (3.39× faster than fast-ssim2, 5.22× faster than margarine), while margarine’s tiled row buffers hold lower 16-thread total-process peak RSS (167,168 KiB vs A’s 598,216 KiB).

8. Known limits, primary records, and citations

Summary of established limits

  • Where published peers rank higher: SSIMULACRA2 edges A on CID22-B (0.913 vs 0.909), MMSPG JPEG XR (0.934 vs 0.920), IRCCyN/IVC (0.949 vs 0.919), LIVE-MD (0.913 vs 0.902), NITS-IQA (0.777 vs 0.734), and KADID pixelation (0.684 vs 0.577); FSIMc leads the 417 codec rows (0.906 vs 0.895) and the Shared-three SROCC mean (0.864 vs 0.850); CVVDP (0.94) and SSIMULACRA2 (0.93) lead CID:IQ SGCK gamut mapping (A: 0.87, F3: 0.91).
  • KADID TERMINAL internal confirmation failed against both registered KADID-trained research checkpoints (B and by_v2fy), despite leading SSIMULACRA2 and Butteraugli on the same 2,000 pairs.
  • Global tone, contrast, and geometric edits remain weak across all tested FR metrics: NITS-IQA D4 contrast change reads 0.22 for A (best tested metric: SSIMULACRA2 0.46); KADID color-block (0.489) and non-eccentricity patch (0.500) show similar local-metric limits.
  • Close learned-codec and ladder ordering: on CLIC 2021 pairs within 2.4 points, A leans toward smoother reconstructions where raters prefer fine detail; 3.39% of close encoder-ladder pairs agreed on by Butteraugli and CVVDP remain misordered despite feature-level monotonicity.
  • Spatial steering vs Butteraugli: one text-screenshot map case reads 0.69 vs 0.70, and closed-loop JPEG XL quantization steered by zensim’s 156-coefficient map uses 2.18%–5.31% more bytes than a Butteraugli-steered loop at matched judge scores.
  • Small images, HDR, and restricted sets: images below 64 px are reflect-padded (where SSIMULACRA2 is invalid); HDR scores depend on declared white/boost and are not a calibrated human scale; committee-only benchmark labels are excluded from public reports.
