# GMSD — port, evaluation, and what zensim can take from it (2026-09-22)

Lane `gmsd`. Machine-readable record: [`gmsd_2026-09-22.json`](gmsd_2026-09-22.json);
full-detail artifacts behind [`gmsd_2026-09-22.pointer.md`](gmsd_2026-09-22.pointer.md).

GMSD — Gradient Magnitude Similarity Deviation (Xue, Zhang, Mou & Bovik, *IEEE
TIP* 23(2), 2014): 2×2 average + decimate, Prewitt gradient magnitude on both
images, per-pixel `GMS = (2·m_r·m_d + c)/(m_r² + m_d² + c)`, score = the
standard deviation of the GMS map. No trained parameters.

## Part 1 — the port (zenmetrics `crates/gmsd`)

zenmetrics commits `53a49715` (crate + `--metric gmsd`), `c59cb81e` (fused SIMD
gray conversion), `051b37af` + `8fc65d91` (integer luma + AVX-512 tier, Devin SWE-2,
gated here), `ce18b182` (consumers enable `avx512`). Record: zenmetrics
`benchmarks/gmsd_parity_2026-09-22.md`. Parity re-run with the AVX-512 tier live: 64/64
maps bit-identical; Part-2 scores moved ≤ 5.8e-16 (111/3,561, the pooling-order fix),
SROCC/KROCC bit-identical.

- **Parity vs libgmsd** (C, MIT, built outside the repo as a differential
  oracle only): 64 real TRAIN pairs, 26×64 … 1024×768 — GMS map **bit-identical
  64/64**, score max |Δ| 9.5e-15 (4.0e-13 relative; one-pass vs two-pass f64
  pooling). Re-checked after the SIMD conversion: still 64/64, and every
  Part-2 GMSD score re-computed bit-identical (3,561/3,561).
- **Constant:** `c = 170` on 0..255, as libgmsd and the authors' `GMSD.m`; the
  paper's `0.0026` is the same constant on 0..1 (`0.0026·255² = 169.065`).
- Odd sizes use libgmsd's documented `⌊w/2⌋×⌊h/2⌋` grid (libgmsd itself writes
  past its buffer there; GMSD.m keeps a half-zero-padded sample).
- archmage/magetypes: one `#[arcane]` entry per tier, `#[rite]` row helpers;
  every tier bit-identical to scalar. Disassembly of the AVX2 entry: row
  helpers inlined, 8-wide `ymm`, no call and no bounds branch in the GMS loop;
  the gray conversion is 4-wide f64 (`vcvtdq2pd/vmulpd/vroundpd/vcvtpd2ps`).
- Output: score (+ map mean, GMSM) and the half-resolution GMS map; strided
  f32 input, sRGB8 input, zenpixels `PixelSlice` input, rayon bands with
  thread-count-independent results.

## Part 3 — what zensim can learn

### What zensim already has

Both of GMSD's ideas are **already in zensim's 944-wide surface**: the v2 block's
`gms` (mean of `1 − GMS`, central-difference gradient, `C_GMS = 1e-4`) and the
append block's `gms_dev2` — the standard deviation of that same GMS map per
scale/channel, i.e. GMSD's deviation pooling — plus `ssim_dev2/ssim_dev4`,
`art_dev2` and `det_dev2` (`feature_defs.rs`, `feature_v2.rs::GMS_DEV2`). The
**228-column basic+peaks surface** — the leaders' recipe and the Rev3 rich
ensemble — has **none** of them. The screen asks whether it should.

Also measured, for the "deviation pooling of existing maps" arm: on the 228
surface the std of the SSIM, edge-artifact and detail-loss maps is already an
exact function of two existing columns (mean and L2: `σ² = n/(n−1)(L2² − μ²)`) —
Spearman of emitted std vs `√(L2² − L1²)` ≥ 0.999996 in every cell. The new
information is the std of the MSE and HF-energy maps.

100,997 TRAIN rows; dev legs safesyn 38,758 / cid22 3,785 / human 993 / codec

verified bit-identical to the zgeom z2_box2 tables on all five legs).

A_gms−A_gmsp `+0.0006 +0.0018 −0.0008 +0.0033 +0.0012`;

Verdicts:
- **GMS-deviation: no gain.** Indistinguishable from G (2/2, |Δ| ≤ 0.002) and
  only +0.0012 over its own permuted control — inside the ±0.002 seed band the
  block5 lane measured on the same recipe.
- **Deviation pooling of the existing maps: harmful as an addition.** The
  columns carry real signal (5/0 over the permuted control, t = 7.4), but 96
  extra columns cost more than they bring (the permuted block alone costs
  −0.0136); net −0.0039, 0/5.
- Neither survives, so no replacement arm was run.

## What zensim should adopt from GMSD

Not a feature. Both of GMSD's ideas are already implemented in zensim's wide
surface, and adding either to the 228-column production surface measured as no
gain (GMS deviation) or a net loss (deviation pooling of the existing maps) on
five paired seeds against size-matched permuted controls. What GMSD does offer
is a **cost bar**: a parameter-free, luma-only index that runs in 0.7 ms per
megapixel on one thread (0.14 ms/MP on eight) and needs < 1 MB beyond its inputs and still beats fast-ssim2 and
zensim D on global SROCC on both admitted human EVAL populations (and B on
KADID, though not on KonFiG, where B leads it 0.819 to 0.759) — while trailing
the trained Rev3 rich ensemble by 0.066 (KADID) and 0.080 (KonFiG), and
trailing zensim on codec content and on colour distortions. That makes GMSD a useful cheap baseline row and a
speed floor to measure zensim's per-feature cost against, not something to
fold in.
