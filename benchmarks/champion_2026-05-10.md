## Recipes

Both share:
- 228 → 192 → 128 → 1 LeakyReLU MLP (two hidden layers)
- Training base: 218k clean safe-synthetic + KADID/TID@humw=0.3
- 200 epochs, batch=16384, lr=3e-3, AdamW
- Trained via `scripts/v_next/train_v_next_mlp.py`

They differ only in TV-weight:
- **Champion**: `--tv-weight 10` (more aggregate quality, less smoothness)
- **Smoothness-Winner**: `--tv-weight 30` (less aggregate, more smoothness)

## Ship recommendation

**Default**: Ship the **Champion** (TV=10).
- KADID gain (+0.082) and TID gain (+0.034) are large, well-replicated.
- The CID22 cost (-0.010) is bounded; aggregate +0.035 is real.
- Smoothness 4.77% beats V0_2 floor.

If smoothness is the dominant priority (e.g. encoder rate-control loops):
swap to **Smoothness-Winner** (TV=30) — non-mono 4.49% at -0.010 aggregate cost.

## Why CID22 didn't reach V0_5's 0.8893

The 2026-04-30 V0_4 mixed-supervision bake (== V0_5 SSIM2-proxy MLP, currently
shipped) was produced by the in-tree Rust `zensim-validate/src/mlp_train.rs`
deleted in PR #29 (commit `e613224`). It used different optimizer and
initialization. The Python `train_v_next_mlp.py` faithfully implements the
loss and architecture but produces -0.010 to -0.020 CID22 SROCC even on
identical training corpus.

Closing the gap is **Phase 4 future work**: port the Rust trainer's exact
recipe. Estimated effort: 1 day (decompose mlp_train.rs from git history,
re-implement Nelder-Mead-style multi-restart in Python).
