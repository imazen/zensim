## 1. The one thing that is varied, and the eight things that are not

Every bake in this record comes from the SAME chain, which is the shipped
Profile-D chain with `--anchor` swapped for `--anchor-parquet` (proven
byte-equivalent in [`d_id100_2026-09-04.md`](d_id100_2026-09-04.md) §1) and the
21-row identity anchor added, exactly as
[`rev2_refit_2026-09-06.md`](rev2_refit_2026-09-06.md) §2 ran it:

```
gram      --parquet <r6b>/tables/<arm>/safesyn.parquet --target human_score \
          --target-scale 0.01 --space raw
fit-lasso --space raw --target human_score --lam 2e-3 --tau 0 --n-sweeps 400 \
          --tol 1e-10 --slice-file a{156,228}.idx \
          --anchor-parquet <r6b>/tables/<arm>/anchor.parquet \
          --anchor-parquet identity_anchor_n21.parquet \
          --anchor-target human_score --embed-repro
extend-top --anchor <r6b>/tables/<arm>/anchor.parquet --target-col human_score
densify   --gate-rows 512
```

`--target-scale 0.01` is not a free choice — it is what makes `--lam 2e-3` mean
in this lane what it means in the shipped recipe. `fit-lasso`'s λ acts on the
mean-loss scale with the FEATURES standardized and the TARGET not, so ρ scales
linearly in `y`; against R6b's ×100 target the same λ lands ~147 active
coefficients instead of the D lineage's 28. That reasoning is the REFIT lane's
(§1 of its record) and its validation is the same: the revision-1 arm lands on
**exactly** the shipped D's active count.

The **identity anchor is arm-independent, and that is measured, not assumed**:
every arm's `identity.parquet` is all-zero on 372 of 372 slots across all 400
self-pairs (control C-C below), so a per-arm 21-row cut is byte-identical to
every other. The REFIT lane's file is reused verbatim, which is also what
byte-reproducing its bakes requires.

**Held constant across arms:** the solver, λ, τ, sweep cap, tolerance, the two
slices, the anchor row set, the identity anchor, the spline fitter, `extend-top`'s
band and knot count, `densify`'s gate size, the eval corpora, the dial
instruments' construction, the floor rule, the tail pins, and the bootstrap's
seed and index sets. **Varied:** the twelve `contrast_inc` slots' arithmetic —
and, in the winsor controls, nothing at all in the features (§4).

## 3. ★ The two revisions do not read the same slots — measured before anything was fitted

`bake_block_profile` on the REFIT lane's own four bakes, intersected with the
twelve F17 ids {12, 25, 38, 51, 64, 77, 90, 103, 116, 129, 142, 155}:

| bake | active coeffs | F17 slots read | which |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| `D_ratio_s156` (revision 1) | 28 | **1** | f77 |
| `D_satexcess_s156` (revision 2) | 33 | **5** | f12, f38, f64, f129, f155 |
| `D_ratio_s228` (revision 1) | 27 | **1** | f77 |
| `D_satexcess_s228` (revision 2) | 39 | **7** | f12, f25, f38, f51, f64, f129, f155 |

**Bounding the statistic does not rescale an input the model already had — it
changes which inputs L1 keeps.** The revision-1 D reads ONE of the twelve; the
revision-2 D reads five or seven, and gains 5–12 active coefficients overall.
An unbounded column with a 36,466-max and a 2.6 %-above-1 tail is, after
standardization, a column whose variance is carried by a handful of rows; L1
drops it. Bound it and it becomes a well-conditioned column L1 keeps.

Two consequences, and they frame everything below:

* **"rev1 vs rev2 in the D chain" is a comparison of two different sparse
  supports**, not of one model with a rescaled input. A single arm's delta
  cannot separate "bounding costs rank here" from "*this* bound costs rank
  here" — which is precisely what the per-arm sweep is for.
* **The winsor control is not the same experiment as a bounded feature.** A
  winsor guard clamps the value the model sees while leaving the *fit's* column
  statistics to whatever the clamp produces — it is a third support again. It is
  run because it answers a different, cheaper question (§4), not because it is a
  substitute.

## 4. The winsor control — three variants, and why the obvious one is wrong

The brief's alternative to changing the feature is to bound the MODEL's input:
`winsor_p99` guards on D's `contrast_inc` inputs, revision-1 features, no era
break, no recalculation, no re-extraction. Shipped Profile B is exactly that
recipe (`b_sdr_linear_cid80_inclwinsor`), and
[`feature_rev2_2026-09-05.md`](feature_rev2_2026-09-05.md) §11.3 is why it is
worth testing here: A and B are fully guarded on their F17 slots and **D, the
SDR default, carries no `feature_transforms` block at all.**

**The literal invocation is not sound, and that was read from the owner's source
before it was run.** `bake_dial_refit add-winsor` writes the transform tokens and
their `[p0.1, p99.9]` windows and then carries *"everything the raw bake had
(incl. its spline) verbatim"* (`cmd_add_winsor`). Clamping inputs changes the
net's raw outputs, so the carried output-calibration spline was fitted on the
**un**-winsorized net — the same hazard the QUANTIZE-then-CALIBRATE rule exists
for, in a different guise. So:

| variant | mechanism | spline |
|---|---|---|
| **`W-all`** | all 372 slots clamped, the clamp INSIDE the fit (`gram --space shaped --transforms-tsv` + the same TSV declared into the bake by `fit-lasso --transforms-tsv`) | fit on the CLAMPED net |
| **`W-f17`** | the twelve F17 slots only, same mechanism — **this is the variant that isolates F17** | fit on the CLAMPED net |
| **`W-all-carried`** | the LITERAL `add-winsor` → `extend-top` → `densify` chain | CARRIED from the un-clamped fit |

`W-all-carried` is graded rather than dropped, so the carry's cost is a measured
number instead of an argument. The windows for all three come from ONE place —
`add-winsor`'s own `[p0.1, p99.9]` fit on the training leg, lifted verbatim out
of its bake's `zentrain.feature_transform_params` metadata into the two screen
TSVs — so the three clamp at identical numbers and differ only in mechanism and
scope. `fit-lasso`'s shaped path applies the same transforms to the anchor rows
before the spline fit (it REFUSES `--space shaped` with `--anchor-parquet` and
no `--transforms-tsv`), so the fit space, the spline and the f32 runtime all see
one clamp.

  with `C4 min` −79.87 → −86.06. The stale-spline defect §4 predicted from the
  owner's source is exactly what §10.2 said it was.
* **The CSIQ gain survives the refit intact** — **+0.04862** at a156, the same
  number to five decimals, because a monotone spline cannot move a rank (G-D).
* **But the `avif-svt` floor loss is NOT the spline. It survives the refit
  unchanged** (0.9744 both ways), so `W-all-refit` is still ineligible under
  §12.7.1 criterion 2. The all-372 guard costs that floor by clamping, not by
  mis-calibrating.
* **And the CSIQ gain is a property of the ALL-372 guard, not of F17.** Scoped to
  the twelve F17 slots CSIQ is a TIE (−0.000004). Whatever `W-all` is fixing on
  CSIQ, it is not `contrast_inc`.

Two-reference inversions (`--inversion-truth agree`, 9,593 reference cells):
**12 of 12 PASS G3 ≤ 0.07** with room — 0.00542 at a156 (identical to revision
1 for every scoped arm; 0.00553 for `W-all-refit`) and 0.03464–0.03496 at a228.

### 11a.9 What is NOT claimed

* **Nothing is installed.** Same list as §11.
* **★ On THIS bake the guard is nearly inert, and that is the honest frame.**
  The shipped-D recipe re-fit on r6b's revision-1 tables reads exactly **ONE**
  of the twelve F17 slots (f77) at both slices. Restricted to the support the
  model actually reads, the p99.9 guard clamps **197 of 196,086** safesyn rows,
  **64 of 779** LIVE rows and **0 of 4,292** CID22 rows. So the CID22 tie is
  close to arithmetically forced. The finding is that the bound is **FREE** in
  this class, not that it is a rank win.
* **The SHIPPED Profile D reads a DIFFERENT support and is UNMEASURED here.**
  `DEFECT_F17`'s exposure table names **f116 and f155** for the shipped ADD156
  bake; this lineage's re-fit reads f77 instead. Applying the same guard +
  spline refit to the shipped bake — which needs its own dial anchor and its own
  instruments — is REGISTERED, not run, and is the obvious next step if the user
  wants the guard on the artefact that actually ships.
* **`W-f17`'s and `W-all`'s numbers are re-used from §6–§9 unchanged**; they were
  not re-run. Only the `ratio` reference series was re-scored (identically).
* **No perf claim.** A declared winsor transform adds a clamp per guarded input
  at inference; it was not measured.
* **The windows are safesyn's**, fitted on the training leg. A different fit
  corpus gives different windows; that sensitivity is still UNMEASURED. What IS
  now measured is the sensitivity to the PERCENTILE, over three values.
* **Confounds inherited unchanged from §11**: the CID22 `image`-crate decoder
  (same on every side), the 504-row KonJND ruler, KADID/TID train==val.
* **Single-fit CIs**, as in §6: deterministic convex solves, no seed.
