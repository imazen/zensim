**Findings (photos):**
1. **The model-coherent maps deliver real RD value.** ADD156+abs-map saves bytes on ALL
   THREE judges (+2.3% even on butteraugli's own judge); winner+signed-map is the best
   ssim2 saver (+4.5%) and positive everywhere. The coherence→RD chain is validated:
   the two drivers built from the 2026-07-18 coherence measurement beat the native
   butteraugli loop on 2 of 3 judges and hold the third.
2. **The incumbent pairing's weakness is visible in bytes**: zensimB_trained (B scalar +
   shipped Trained map — measured steer coherence 0.243) wins ssim2 but REGRESSES the
   butteraugli judge (−0.7%) — the gaming pattern the judge panel exists to catch.
   Swapping in its model map (zensimB_model_abs) fixes butteraugli (+0.5%) at an ssim2
   cost; the basic-input drivers don't have to make that trade.
3. **Screen content: every zensim-scalar driver LOSES bytes** (−1 to −21%) while native
   butteraugli gains (+8.6% butter). n=2 images — small — but consistent with the loop's
   photo-seeded distance-target table and zensim's known non-photo behavior. Do not drive
   screen content with these zensim loops as-is; this is the sharpest next-work signal.

**Efficiency (jxl, median encode_ms):** all perceptual loops cost ~3× the plain encode
(photo 276–306ms vs 93ms; screen ~3.0s vs 0.72s); zensim-loop cost ≈ butteraugli-loop cost
(within 5%) — the metric choice is free at equal iteration count; the loop itself is the cost.

\* cross-scale: targeted the ADD156 dial, judged on the zensim-judge scale.

**Findings (zenjpeg):**
4. **JPEG per-block AQ modulation is worthless as wired**: `aq` ≡ `global` to the byte
   (+0.0 everywhere) — the AQ-strength lever only nudges zero-bias rounding (measured:
   20% strength change → byte-identical). Per-block steering for JPEG needs a real rate
   lever (per-block quant-field modulation, jxl-style) — the structural follow-up.
5. **The measured loop's value over one-shot is precision, not bytes**: global's med
   residual 0.84 vs picker's 4.33 — 5× tighter targeting for 3× the encode time
   (4 passes/192ms vs 1 pass/60ms). Quality-per-byte at equal judged score is ~equal.
   That is the closed-loop-vs-one-shot tradeoff, quantified: pay ~130ms to know you hit T.
6. **ADD156 as scalar+map gives small consistent gains** (+0.5–1.4% across judges/classes)
   over the B-driven baseline — the only zenjpeg driver positive on screens.

## Caveats (honest gaps)

- Screen class n=2 images; photo n=8. Probe scale — directions are trustworthy, single
  percents are not; the full-corpus phase (design §phasing) must precede any shipped
  constant.
- The judge zensim profile is whatever the current zenmetrics build carries — uniform
  across drivers (valid comparisons), but its absolute scale isn't the loop's B.
- Both codecs' calibration tables (zenjpeg starting-q, jxl zensim_targets) are still
  legacy-V0_2-seeded (the `834b4387` fix changed loop scoring); re-seeding will improve
  convergence (fewer passes), not the equal-quality byte comparisons.
- jxl per-driver iteration counts fixed at 4 (no early-exit tuning); the winner bake ran
  raw-scale (its per-image gradient is what mattered); zenjpeg winner-as-scalar was
  excluded (raw scale can't take dial targets — see runner note).
