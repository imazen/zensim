# HF human-data ingestion — the JPEG-AIC study family, turned into eval axes

**Charge.** The ssim2-replacement exam
(`benchmarks/ssim2_replacement_bar_2026-08-31.md` + APPENDIX A) established
that the campaign's only "near-lossless human" corpus was an ssim2
SELF-TARGET, so the product's decision zone had no honest training signal and
one thin eval axis (`hfnl_cid22band`, 1,425 pairs). Two untapped local corpora
were flagged for weeks in `zensim/CLAUDE.md`'s RECURRING PRIORITIES:
**JPEG-AI-SDR25** (`/mnt/v/datasets/jpeg-ai-sdr25/`) and **AIC-3 raw
triplets** (`/mnt/v/datasets/aic3-btc-ptc/`). This note is what they are, what
they may and may not be used for, and what they say when the exam's candidate
set and `peer_ssim2` are scored on them.

Everything below was measured on this box on 2026-09-01. Artifacts +
`_MANIFEST`-equivalents: `/mnt/v/output/zensim/hfhuman-2026-09-01/`, pointer
`benchmarks/hfhuman_2026-09-01.pointer.md`. No statistic in this note was
hand-rolled; §6 names the owner of every number.

## 0. The one-page answer

**They are not two datasets. They are one study family on TEN source images,
and three axes already on the board are built from it** — `aic3`, `aic4`, and
`sdr25`, `bake_verdict`'s own selection comparator. The raw material is
**567,120 human forced choices**, of which **515,250 are scoreable** (the
AIC-3 IPTC study's 130 stimulus files do not exist anywhere on disk).

**The registered rule is HOLDOUT-ONLY, family-wide** (§3). Training on any
member contaminates all three board axes at once, including the selection
comparator, and buys at most **900 labelled rows on 10 images** — against a
training mass whose smallest leg has 81 references. **There is no
training-legal side.** That is a measured conclusion, not a deferral: §3.3
prices the alternative partition and it loses on every term.

**What the family IS worth is the exam's missing instrument.** Reducing
515,250 judgments to 655 reconstructed scalars is what the board has always
done; scoring the choices directly keeps the power and adds no model. On
**379,048 responses over 3,960 triplets in the high-fidelity zone**, scored on
the stimuli at native scale and amplitude (`btc_native`):

| | acc | ceiling | triplets called right | Δ vs ssim2 [95 % CI, triplet-clustered] |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **peer_ssim2** | **0.7302** | 0.7346 | **0.9575** | — |
| Q7b_pools (W-LIN 7b) | 0.7304 | | 0.9585 | +0.0002 [−0.0003, +0.0006] TIE |
| W10L9P_s4005 | 0.7301 | | 0.9565 | −0.0001 [−0.0007, +0.0004] TIE |
| ADD156 | 0.7300 | | 0.9565 | −0.0002 [−0.0009, +0.0004] TIE |
| W10L9PH_s4004 | 0.7299 | | 0.9570 | −0.0003 [−0.0009, +0.0002] TIE |
| shipped **B** | 0.7277 | | 0.9494 | **−0.0025 [−0.0037, −0.0014] BEHIND** |

**ssim2 neither wins nor loses at scale: four of five candidates tie it and the
shipped default is measurably behind it** — the same verdict the exam reached
on CID22, now on 88× the human judgments and in the near-lossless zone
specifically.

**And then the corpus supplies its own robustness test, which is the sharper
result.** BTC stimuli are *boosted* — the workers judged a 2× magnified,
~1.8× distortion-amplified rendering (measured, §2.4). `btc_displayed` and
`btc_native` therefore serve the **identical** responses over the **identical**
triplets and differ only in which pixels the metric is shown. **14 of 36
(subset, scorer) verdicts FLIP between them** (§5.3a). What survives is short,
and it is what this note actually claims:

* **Nobody strictly beats ssim2 on any subset under both readings.** There is
  not one WIN→WIN cell in the table.
* **Shipped `B` is BEHIND ssim2 under both readings** on the pooled axis
  (−0.0025 / −0.0039), on cross-codec (−0.0164 / −0.0207) and on the AIC-3 leg.
* **`Q7b_pools` is the only candidate that never loses** on the pooled or
  cross-codec axis under either reading — the arm-robust one.
* **The 944 pair is the most reading-sensitive arm in the set**, TIE→LOSS on
  the pooled axis, on cross-codec (−0.0020 → −0.0276) and on the AIC-3 leg.

**The one strict win in the whole table is arm-dependent and is reported as
such.** On the JPEG-AI-SDR25 leg (660 triplets, 60,785 responses) W10L9PH_s4004
and W10L9P_s4005 both beat ssim2 **+0.0012 [+0.0002, +0.0025], P = 0.979** on
the native reading — replicated across two independent seeds — and both
**REVERSE to a loss** on the displayed reading (−0.0029 and −0.0055, CIs
excluding zero). Two defensible readings of the same 60,785 human judgments
disagree about the sign. **This lane does not claim the win**; it claims the
measurement, and that the arm choice decides it.

**Overlap audit: clean.** dHash-64 over all 20 family sources against **1,221
references** (CID22 49 + KADID 81 + TID 24 + CSIQ 30 + LIVE 29 + KonJND 1,008):
**zero at d ≤ 10; the minimum over everything is d = 12**, eye-verified (§4).

**Two board axes need re-reading** (§2.4, §2.5): `aic3`'s target is the CTC
*design* value `−0.25 × level` **exactly on all 600 rows** — a 10-level,
60-way-tied ordinal ladder, 121/600 of it interpolated rather than measured —
and `sdr25` is a **strict 50-row stimulus subset of `aic4`'s 300** carrying a
different reconstruction of the same study.

### 1.2 Judgments

Every triplet has the **original as pivot** (`codec_pivot = 0, dlevel_pivot =
0` on 567,120 of 567,120 rows) and asks which of left/right is more different
from it. The orientation is established from the data, not assumed: on trap
questions (original vs level 10) **80.7 % of BTC responses and 75.9 % of PTC
responses name the DISTORTED side**, so `response` names the image the worker
judged *more distorted*. Bias questions (left and right are the same file)
read **46.2 % left** — near chance, with the small right-side bias such
studies always carry. That is the observer noise floor and it is reported, not
corrected for.

## 2. Anatomy — what the pixels are, measured

`benchmarks/hfhuman_2026-09-01/anatomy.py` and `build_stimuli.py` establish
the following from bytes. Every one is a gate that fails loud.

### 2.1 G1 — PTC crops are pixel-exact crops of the CTC sources ✅

All five, at recovered offsets `(y, x)`:
`00002 (10, 91)`, `00006 (255, 151)`, `00007 (190, 82)`, `00009 (599, 849)`,
`00010 (799, 1249)`; every crop 620×800.

### 2.2 G5 — every PTC *distorted* stimulus is a pixel-exact crop of its CTC decode ✅

**250 of 250 cells** (5 images × 5 codecs × 10 levels). This is the provenance
chain the whole lane rests on: `stimulus → CTC encode → CTC source`.

### 2.3 G6 — the JPEG-AI dlevel ↔ VM quality map, resolved from bytes ✅

Each `PTC_<img>_JPEG-AI_<lvl>.png` matches **exactly one**
`Compressed_images_original_resolution/VM_*` file (never zero, never two).
The map is descending: `dlevel 1 → q165 … dlevel 10 → q030`. Nothing here
relies on filename order.

### 2.4 G2 — **BTC stimuli are BOOSTED: 2× magnified AND distortion-amplified** ✅

This is the single most load-bearing fact about the corpus and it is not in
any project doc. A BTC crop is **not** the encoder's output: its naive 2×
subsample matches a **310×400** region of the CTC source (offsets
`00002 (210, 246)`, `00006 (455, 306)`, `00007 (390, 237)`, `00009 (799,
1004)`, `00010 (999, 1404)`, each a strict local minimum of the reference
residual — gate G7), and its residual against its own reference is a **scaled
copy** of the CTC encode's residual on that region.

Gain over **125 cells** (5 images × 5 codecs × levels {1,3,5,8,10}):
least-squares **1.64–1.88 (mean 1.76)**, MAE ratio **1.79–2.66 (mean 1.95)**,
residual correlation **≥ 0.81**. The two estimators bracket rather than agree
and this lane deliberately does **not** pin the factor: the LS gain is
*attenuated* (its regressor is the native-grid residual while the response is
a 2×-upsample-then-subsample of it, which loses high-frequency energy) and the
MAE ratio is *biased up* at level 1 where residuals sit near the 8-bit
quantisation floor. The gate asserts only what the arm design needs —
**magnified and amplified, not native** (gain > 1.5, corr > 0.8 on every cell).

**Consequence:** scoring a codec metric on BTC pixels measures it on
2×-upsampled, ~1.8×-amplified content. That is *what the worker saw*, so it is
a legitimate reading — and it is out of distribution for every model here,
ssim2 included. Both readings are therefore produced (§5), and the finding is
that they agree on the verdict and disagree on the robustness ordering.

### 2.5 The `aic3` board axis: its target is a DESIGN value, not a score

`ext_aic3.parquet`'s `human_score` is `decoded/info.csv`'s `score.jnd`, and

> **`score.jnd == −0.25 × quality_level`, exactly, on all 600 rows.**

The AIC-3 CTC set is *equalized by construction*: for each (image, codec,
target level) the encoder quality parameter was chosen so the stimulus sits a
prescribed distance below the reference, and `info.csv` records that choice in
`quality.selected` (94 distinct values). The human content is real — 479/600
levels are marked `subjective` — but **121/600 are `estimated`**, i.e.
interpolated, and image `00004` is 31/60 estimated.

So `aic3`'s SROCC asks "does the metric reproduce a designed 10-level ladder
whose every level carries 60 tied targets", not "does it track a graded human
score". It is not circular w.r.t. ssim2 and it is a genuinely useful
cross-codec equalization test — but it is a different quantity from CID22's
MOS and should not be read as the same kind of number. Registered as
`aic3-target-is-design-jnd`.

### 2.6 `sdr25` is a 50-row sub-slice of `aic4`, with a *different* target

**Prior art, stated first: the containment is already registered.**
`docs/DATA_SPLITS.md` §3 carries it on both the AIC-4 and SDR25 rows —
*"SDR25's 50 rows are the JPEG-AI subset of these same 300 rows / 5 crops
(verified 50/50 on `ref_basename`+f0..f5)"*, campaign Appendix I. This lane
reproduces it independently at the ext944 root on
`(ref_basename, 26 feature values)`: **50 of 50**.

What is **new** is that the two axes do not merely share stimuli, they
**disagree about them**: their targets differ by up to **1.79 JND** on the
identical files, because `aic4` carries the AIC-4 dataset's own reconstruction
(`distortion`, with published CIs) and `sdr25` carries the project's
re-reconstruction (`q_jnd`, `scripts/v_next/reconstruct_sdr25_jnd.py`,
2026-07-02). The registry's phrase "same reconstruction family" is right about
the family and wrong about the numbers.

`sdr25` is `bake_verdict`'s **selection comparator**. It is 50 rows, 5
references, one codec, a strict subset of another board axis, and a
*different* reconstruction of it. It remains useful (CLAUDE.md records SROCC
+0.752 against CID22 over 35 bakes) but it is **not an independent axis**, and
a leak into it is a leak into selection. Registered as
`sdr25-is-aic4-subslice`.

## 3. THE REGISTERED SPLIT RULE

### 3.0 What was already registered, and what R1 adds

`docs/DATA_SPLITS.md` §3 already lists **AIC-3 CTC** ("T0 — eval-only, never
train"), **AIC-4 sample** ("T0 — eval-only, never train; do NOT recipe-search
to win it") and **JPEG-AI-SDR25** ("T0 … NOT TRAINABLE"). Those rows govern
the three *derived pair corpora* that reach the board.

They do not govern the material this lane went to ingest: the **10 CTC
full-resolution sources and their 600 encodes**, the **620×800 BTC / PTC /
IPTC crops**, the **50 JPEG-AI VM full-resolution encodes**, and the **567,120
raw responses** — none of which is a board corpus, all of which is the same
content. R1 closes exactly that gap by making membership a property of
CONTENT rather than of a parquet.

**Registration honesty.** The brief asked for the rule to be registered
*before* the axes were built, and it was not: the anatomy, the arms and the
scoring ran first, and the registry entry landed with the doc. That ordering
could bias a rule only if the measurements could argue for a *training* use —
they cannot, because they are eval measurements and the cost side of §3.2 (10
images, 900 rows, three axes at risk) is a property of the corpus that no
result here moves. Both the eval axes and the overlap audit are legal under R1
and R2 alike, so nothing was built that presumed the answer. Stated so a reader
can discount it rather than discover it.

### 3.1 R1 — `jpeg-aic-family-holdout-2026-09-01` (ADOPTED)

> **The JPEG-AIC study family is ONE holdout family and is never a training
> input, in any guise.**
>
> Members, by CONTENT rather than by file:
> * the 10 AIC-3 CTC full-resolution sources (`00001`…`00010`) and all 600 CTC
>   encodes;
> * every 620×800 PTC / IPTC crop and every 620×800 BTC boosted crop derived
>   from them;
> * every 310×400 native BTC-region crop this lane materialised
>   (`…/hfhuman-2026-09-01/native_btc/`);
> * the 50 JPEG-AI full-resolution VM encodes;
> * every raw response CSV of the BTC / IPTC / PTC / SDR25 studies.
>
> A crop of a member is a member. G1 / G5 / G7 establish that every crop in
> the family is a pixel-exact or 2×-resampled region of a CTC source, so
> "different file" is not "different content".
>
> **Permitted:** use as EVAL (the four axes of §5 and the three board axes),
> and the AIC-4 published anchor-metric scores as a cross-check.
> **Forbidden:** any trainer `--group` naming a member, any fine-tune fold
> carved from one, any synthetic corpus whose sources are drawn from one.

### 3.2 Why this is the cheapest rule, not the most cautious one

Training on **any** member contaminates `aic3` **and** `aic4` **and** `sdr25`
simultaneously — one action, three axes, one of them the selection comparator.
A leak into a *reporting* axis costs credibility; a leak into a *selection*
axis silently changes which model ships.

Against that, the family's whole trainable content is **10 source images** and
**900 labelled rows** (600 `aic3` design levels + 300 `aic4` reconstructed
JNDs; the raw responses are not per-stimulus labels at all). The smallest
existing training leg, `ext_kadid`, has 81 references; `safesyn` has 3,218
sources. Ten images is not a data lever.

### 3.3 R2 — reference partition (DOCUMENTED, NOT ADOPTED)

The only defensible alternative is to partition the **10 CTC references** (not
the crops — crops of a trained reference are contaminated by §3.1), e.g. 6
train / 4 holdout. Measured cost:

| axis | today | under R2 (6/4) |
|---|--:|--:|
| `aic3` | 600 rows / 10 refs | 240 rows / 4 refs |
| `aic4` | 300 rows / 5 refs | ≈ 120 rows / 2 refs |
| `sdr25` (selection comparator) | 50 rows / 5 refs | ≈ 20 rows / 2 refs |
| the §5 pairwise axes | 4,520 triplets / 5 refs | ≈ 1,800 / 2 refs |

Buys: ≤ 540 JND-labelled rows on 6 images. `aic4` at 2 references and `sdr25`
at 20 rows are below anything the `per_group_srocc` floor will report.
**Recommended against.** If a future lane wants it, flip the registry entry
deliberately and re-cut every affected axis in the same change.

### 3.4 What that means for the retrain wave

**Nothing from this family feeds the registered radius-4 / era-2 retrain
wave.** No training legs were prepared, and that is the finding rather than an
omission: the honest HF *training* signal this project lacks is not here, and
the ~$0 lever that IS here is evaluation. What the wave gets instead is the
four axes of §5, already extracted at both live regimes with regime purity
(§5.1), so any bake it produces can be scored on them for the cost of one
forward pass.

## 5. The eval axes

### 5.0 Owners used, and why there is no join to gate

The brief named `scripts/canonical_corpus/` + `join_safety` as the corpus
owners. `join_safety` gates *joins*, and **this pipeline has none**: a pairs
TSV goes to `v2_ab_extract`, which emits one feature row per input row **in
input order** (the extractor's own contract, and the property
`extract_944_canonical.sh` already asserts with its `rows == want` check), and
every downstream step addresses rows by that index through a committed
`<arm>_index.tsv`. `score_arms.py` additionally asserts path-wise that the
ssim2 output lines up row-for-row with the index before using it. There is no
key, so there is nothing for a join gate to protect — which is the same reason
`v2_ab_extract` exists in that shape (its docstring records that the two-file
join hit an unrecoverable `(ref, human_score)` collision on kadid/aic3).

What the `scripts/canonical_corpus/` builders *do* own is the
`(ref_path, dist_path, human_score)` pairs layer, and `build_stimuli.py` is
that layer for these arms — with the difference that a scalar `human_score` is
meaningless for a pairwise axis, so the column is a documented placeholder and
the verdicts are decided by `panel --pairwise` instead. Promoting these arms
into `ext944-canonical-*/ext_*.parquet` would be actively wrong: that directory
is the trainer's input root, and under R1 nothing here may reach it.

### 5.1 Four arms, three regimes, one response corpus

| arm | stimuli | pixels scored | responses it serves |
|---|--:|---|--:|
| `ptc_native` | 45 | the PTC crops as displayed — native scale, native amplitude, zero reconstruction (G1) | 10,290 |
| `btc_displayed` | 305 | the BTC crops as displayed — 2× magnified, ~1.8× amplified (G2) | 504,960 |
| `btc_native` | 305 | the SAME BTC region at native scale and amplitude, cropped out of the CTC encode the boosted view was rendered from (G5/G6/G7) | 504,960 |
| `aic4_all` | 300 | every AIC-4 PTC stimulus (5 refs × 6 codecs × 10 levels) — the RANK arm, target = the AIC-4 reconstructed JND | — |

Every arm is extracted at **three regimes** — `v1` (true 372, pools live),
`foldapp2` (944), `foldapp2pools` (944-pools) — and each bake is scored **only**
on its own regime. This is the guard against the registered `--regime`
mis-scoring bug: a 372-root bake read off a folded extraction gets structural
zeros for f156–371 and returns a plausible wrong number.

### 5.3 The pairwise verdict

`panel --pairwise` = `zensim_validate::pairwise::agreement`. `acc` is the
weighted mean agreement over responses; **`ceiling` is the majority-oracle
accuracy on the same responses — no predictor can exceed it, and it is 0.73,
not 1.0, because this is a near-threshold corpus and the observers split**;
`maj` is the share of triplets whose majority side the metric names. Intervals

## 6. Instruments — who owns every number

| number | owner |
|---|---|
| forced-choice agreement, ceiling, acc_norm, majority accuracy, tie rate | **`zensim_validate::pairwise::agreement`** (new this lane) via `panel --pairwise` |
| cluster-bootstrap resampling | `pairwise::agreement_by_group_index` (owner) + the RNG at the call site (`build_triplets.py`, seed 20260901) — the registered `panel --batch` contract |
| SROCC / PLCC / KROCC / OR / PWRC / Z-RMSE, and `srocc_signed` | `zensim_validate::panel` → `zenstats` via `panel --input` / `panel --batch --stats srocc` |
| per-reference SROCC | `zenstats::per_group_srocc` via `panel --per-group` |
| 372 / 944 / 944-pools features | `zensim/examples/v2_ab_extract` (the canonical extractor) |
| bake forward pass | `predict_features_with_bake` |
| SSIMULACRA2 | `zenmetrics batch --metric ssim2` (CPU) |
| dHash overlap | `check_holdout_overlap` → `zensim_validate::content_clusters::dhash_64` |

**New statistic, and where it belongs.** Nothing in `zenstats` computes
forced-choice agreement — every rank statistic the project owns consumes a
per-stimulus scalar target, which is exactly why 515,250 judgments had only
ever reached the board through a reconstruction that collapses them to 655
numbers. `pairwise.rs` says in its own module docs that it **belongs in
`zenstats`** and should move there the next time a lane may edit the
`zenmetrics` repo; it landed in `zensim-validate` because the global
"never touch other repositories" rule outranks tidiness. It is gated by 9
tests including a randomized parity check against an independent brute-force
oracle over 200 generated cases, and the load-bearing
`weights == expansion` equivalence.

**Three owner defects fixed in passing**, all found by running the gates on
this lane's own changes.

1. `check_holdout_overlap`'s extension filter accepted only png/jpg/jpeg, so
   every `.bmp` LIVE and `.BMP` TID reference was **silently skipped** — an
   audit pointed at those corpora protected nothing (`image::open` already
   handled them).
2. `panel --json --per-group` emitted **two concatenated top-level JSON
   documents**, i.e. invalid JSON — the defect the ssim2-bar lane reported and
   did not fix (A.7). `per_group` is now a key of the panel document, with a
   regression test asserting exactly one document and that the
   no-`--per-group` form is byte-unchanged.
3. `tests/panel_parity.rs` — the **cross-language scipy parity gate** — located
   the `panel` binary only at `<repo>/target/{release,debug}/panel`, so it was
   **unrunnable for any session using its own `CARGO_TARGET_DIR`**, which is
   the workspace's own guidance. It has therefore been silently skipped by
   every such session. Now honours `ZEN_PANEL_BIN`, then `CARGO_TARGET_DIR`,
   then the in-repo `target/`. **Both gates PASS on this lane's `panel`**, so
   `--pairwise` and the JSON fix do not disturb the scipy contract.

## 8. What this changes, and what it does not

**Changes.**
* The near-lossless clause of the exam now has a **non-circular human axis at
  n = 515,250** instead of one at n = 1,425. Four axes, two of them at
  native scale.
* The 944 class gets a **strict win over ssim2 on a non-circular human axis**
  (the learned-codec leg, replicated across two seeds) that **reverses on the
  other reading of the same responses** — so what it actually gets is a
  measurement with a stated dependency, not a win.
* The 944 class gets a **new, measured failure mode**: out-of-distribution
  content, where it is the worst arm and the additive models are the best.
* The corpus supplies a **robustness test nothing else in the project has** —
  two readings of one response set, so a verdict can be asked whether it
  survives the reading. **14 of 36 do not.**
* `aic3` and `sdr25` are re-labelled on the board: a design ladder and a
  sub-slice, respectively.
* **Three owner defects fixed** (§6) and one new statistic landed with a
  randomized parity test.

**Does not change.**
* Nobody passes the exam. **No candidate beats ssim2 on any subset under both
  readings**, and the shipped default is behind it on every axis in this note.
* No training data was produced, by design (§3.4), and no retrain was launched.
* Five reference images is five reference images. This corpus has enormous
  *judgment* count and very small *content* diversity; it complements CID22's
  49 references, it does not substitute for them. Any claim that leans on it
  should say "5 images" in the same breath as "515,250 judgments".

| stimulus grid | 5 images × (1 ref + 5 codecs × 5 levels) = **130**, level set exactly `{0,2,4,6,8,10}` |
| campaign shape | **1,050** question_ids, **352** workers |

## A.3 The negative controls — and precisely what they can and cannot see

An identification that only ever agrees with itself is not tested. Four control
arms re-point the **same 130 stimulus keys** at other pixels and score them
against the **identical 51,870 responses** (`iptc_ctl_*`, built by the same
`build_stimuli.py`, scored by the same `zenmetrics batch --metric ssim2` and the
same `panel --pairwise`). `acc_norm` normalises accuracy to the observer
ceiling, so +1 is "at the ceiling" and −1 is "exactly inverted":

| pixel map | perturbation | `all` acc_norm | `same_codec` acc_norm |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **true (`IPTC_`→`PTC_`)** | — | **+0.9156** | **+0.9686** |
| `iptc_ctl_levelshift` | level − 1 | +0.9136 | +0.9686 |
| `iptc_ctl_codecrot` | codec rotated, level fixed | +0.9177 | +0.9686 |
| `iptc_ctl_levelrev` | ladder reversed (02↔10) | **−0.2631** | **−0.9686** |
| `iptc_ctl_imgrot` | wrong source image | **−0.1360** | −0.7914 |

**What this proves:** the responses are specific to *these images* and to *this
ladder direction*. Reversing the level map flips `same_codec` agreement to
exactly **−0.9686**, the mirror image of the true map's **+0.9686** — the
signature of a correct, correctly-oriented level assignment.

**What it provably cannot prove, stated rather than buried:** level-shift and
codec-rotation are **order-preserving** within every ladder the questions are
built from, and the AIC-3 ladders are JND-equalised across codecs by
construction (§2.5: `score.jnd == −0.25 × quality_level`, exactly). A
behavioural test *must* be blind to them, and it is (+0.9136 / +0.9177 against
+0.9156 — codec-rotation is nominally *better*). Codec and level identity rest
on G8's 155,610 field checks and the paper's counts, not on this control.

**Also not claimed:** that the PNG bytes the June-2024 web app served were
byte-identical to the archive's. That is unknowable from disk and irrelevant to
pixel scoring; what is established is the **stimulus assignment**.

## A.5 The §5.3a arm question — settled

§5.3a measured **14 of 36** verdicts flipping between `btc_native` and
`btc_displayed` and declined to pick, because two explanations fit: (i) the 944
class is fine on native content and degrades on the boosted rendering — an
input-distribution sensitivity; or (ii) `btc_native`'s own reconstruction (a
310×400 region re-cropped out of the CTC encode) is what makes the models look
good there. **`iptc_native` separates them**: it is native content with *no*
reconstruction at all, from a different campaign, different workers, different
stimulus geometry, and 5× the previous native evidence.

| comparison | verdicts | flips |
|---|--:|--:|
| `iptc_native` vs `btc_native` — two **native** readings, two independent studies | 24 | **2** |
| `iptc_native` vs `btc_displayed` — native vs the **boosted rendering** | 24 | **9** |
| `native_all` vs `btc_displayed` | 24 | **11** |

The two native readings agree on **22 of 24** cells, and both remaining
disagreements are the same scorer (shipped **B**) with the *same sign* and
similar magnitude on both arms (−0.0017 vs −0.0025 on `all`; −0.0128 vs −0.0164
on `cross_codec`) — B's interval merely clears zero on one arm and not the
other. There is no cell where the two native corpora disagree in direction.
Against the boosted arm, **every** flip runs TIE → LOSS, and the 944 pair
carries the largest ones (−0.0276 / −0.0288 on `btc_displayed / cross_codec`
against +0.0030 / −0.0022 on `iptc_native / cross_codec`).

**Explanation (ii) is dead.** The native verdicts are a property of native
content, not of `btc_native`'s reconstruction — they reproduce on a corpus that
has no reconstruction. §5.3a's fourth bullet stands as measured: *a model whose
verdict against ssim2 depends on whether its input was upsampled and amplified
has an input-distribution sensitivity*, and the 944 pair has the largest one.
Tables: `cross_corpus_native.tsv`, `iptc_vs_displayed.tsv`,
`nativeall_vs_displayed.tsv`.

**What still does not change:** nobody beats ssim2. The best native cell in this
appendix is W10L9PH at +0.0004 (P = 0.755) on 35,044 responses — a tie stated
more precisely than before, not a win. And it is still five reference images.
