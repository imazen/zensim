Reading: ΔMCOS ≈ 2 × ΔTSBPC (so MCOS units ≈ 2 × Elo wins majority).
**Operational implication**: 10 MCOS points = "clear majority" in
pairwise comparisons (ΔTSBPC=5, 96.8% agreement). 5 MCOS points = "narrow
majority" (ΔTSBPC≈2.5, 70-77% agreement). 2 MCOS points = noise floor
(ΔTSBPC≈1, 54-65% agreement).

This bounds *meaningful* score differences for the zensim user-facing
dial — a 2-point shift is below human discrimination; a 10-point shift
is robust.

## Quality-band definitions for zensim reporting

Per Table 5 + the paper's continuous MCOS range (Figure 3 shows 30-93):

| Band | MCOS / zensim score range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **B0: below medium** | < 50 | Obvious distortion |
| **B1: medium** | 50 ≤ s < 65 | Visible artifacts |
| **B2: high** | 65 ≤ s < 90 | Subtle artifacts |
| **B3: visually lossless** | ≥ 90 | No visible difference |

Plus a finer cut around the perceptibility threshold:

| Sub-band | MCOS / zensim score range | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Near-PJND | 58 ≤ s ≤ 68 | KonJND-1k mean PJND = 63-65 ± 5 (Table 4) |

## Per-band reporting rule (for the eval harness)

**Every CID22/KADID/TID/KonJND eval MUST report per-band metrics**, not
just aggregate SROCC:

For each (model, dataset) pair:
1. Per-band SROCC (Spearman rank correlation within each band)
2. Per-band MAE (mean absolute prediction error, in score units)
3. Per-band non-monotonic-q-step rate (within-curve adjacent-q
   reversals, segmented by which band the lower-q pair is in)
4. Per-band sample count (n)

The aggregate SROCC hides band-specific failures. A model with
aggregate SROCC 0.89 can still be 0.95 in B3 (visually lossless) and
0.65 in B1 (medium quality) — that's a different product than 0.85
across all bands.

## Plot styles to replicate from the paper

The paper uses several distinctive chart styles for objective-metric
evaluation. The zensim eval harness should produce equivalents:

1. **Figure 3** — MCOS distribution histogram, stacked by codec.
   For zensim: zensim score histogram, stacked by codec, with the
   B0/B1/B2/B3 vertical band cuts.

2. **Figure 8** — Median MCOS vs bpp curves per encoder.
   For zensim: median predicted score vs bpp per encoder, with
   visually-lossless band highlighted at top.

3. **Figure 9** — p5 MCOS vs bpp (worst-case 5th percentile).
   For zensim: same axes, p5 score vs bpp — surfaces "surprise"
   encoder behavior.

4. **Figure 10** — Visual consistency: MCOS-stdev vs Mean MCOS, per encoder.
   For zensim: predicted-score-stdev vs mean predicted score, per
   encoder. Y-axis inverted (lower stdev = more consistent = better).

5. **Figure 11** — Per-category encoder curves (15 image categories).
   For zensim: per-content-class encoder curves (we have 5 zenanalyze
   content classes; subset matching `category` from CID22 paper's
   15 categories is content-class-mapped).

6. **Figure 13** — 2D histogram of metric-score vs MCOS, with purple
   "expected at PJND" line and percentile contours.
   For zensim: 2D histogram of zensim score vs CID22 MCOS, plus PJND
   horizontal line at score ≈ 63.

## Plot color/typography conventions

- Encoder colors (from Figure 11 legend): JPEG black, MozJPEG black,
  JPEG XL red/pink, WebP green, AVIF orange/yellow, HEIC teal.
- Reference (lossless) shown as blue horizontal band at MCOS 88-92.
- Confidence intervals as error bars; 90% CI by default.
- Categories as small multiples (3-column grid).
- Y-axis labels: "Median MCOS (p25 — p75; p90)" or similar percentile
  band notation.

Per-band cuts (B0/B1/B2/B3) should be shown as gridded horizontal
guides on score axes throughout.
